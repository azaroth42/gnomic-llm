The engine sends the rules with its first message.

You see exactly what an LLM player sees: the Engine message tab is the message an LLM would get, and Rules of the match is its briefing. The rest of the page draws the same information.

The table and the log follow the game live. The engine asks you to act on your turn and, while you own a rule, after every other player's play so you can judge it.

Your reply is built from your choices in the reply format the engine expects. Tick Edit the reply as text to type it yourself. A reply the engine can't use gets an INVALID message; after the same number of retries an LLM gets, the engine plays a random card for you or counts your call as NONE.

Your hypotheses: for every rule you don't own, write your current best statement of exactly what it says, as a rule of its own (e.g. No red card on a red card, A Queen may be played on anything). Changes go with your next reply, as an LLM's do. The engine turns each hypothesis into code and scores how soon and how closely it matches the real rule. You aren't scored on your own rules, and you never see scores during the match. Leave a box empty for "no idea yet".

Notes are your private notes, as an LLM keeps them. They are sent with every reply. Nobody at the table sees them; spectators of the match do.

Keys: Ctrl/⌘+Enter sends.
